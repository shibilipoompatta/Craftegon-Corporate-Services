Statutory Provisions

Current Legal Framework & Regulatory Mandate

When an application is flagged by the Trade Marks Registry, an Examination Report is issued raising statutory objections under the Trade Marks Act, 1999.

Statutory grounds:
  • Section 9 Objections (Absolute Grounds): Refusal based on lack of distinctiveness, descriptive character of goods/services, or generic industry terminology. Rebutted via evidence of acquired distinctiveness and commercial usage.
  • Section 11 Objections (Relative Grounds): Refusal based on phonetic, visual, or conceptual identity/similarity with an earlier cited registered mark. Rebutted via difference in customer base, market channels, and visual differences.
  • Form TM-MISC Written Reply: Detailed legal submission accompanied by turnover certificates, marketing collateral, invoices, and relevant High Court / Supreme Court precedents.
  • Show Cause Hearing: Scheduled if written submissions require verbal substantiation before the Assistant Registrar.
